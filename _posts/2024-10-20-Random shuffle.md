A lot on my mind right now... 

Life is inherently chaotic and confusing. To say otherwise is denial.

I love learning, and value truth and understanding. To me knowledge is important. I do feel a kind of overwhelm or exhaustion... information or narrative overload. I think I'm more curious about understanding myself than I am about reading someone's packaged narrative neatly tying together the latest in biology research, or whatever. 

It goes back to my rejection of philosophy... like music theory, it is analyzing something after it has occurred. For sure, it is important to reflect upon one's life, and upon our collective lives together. That is fundamental. I do think the Buddhists have it most right, or at least my understanding of it. Which is that our experience of our lives, or at least the suffering, boils down to that core feeling of being unworthy.

There is an interesting tension in myself about being worthy. Sometimes I feel like an absolute god in all the things I do, because I do so much and I do it well. 

I wonder if this writing is actually interesting to anyone else, or if it is just kind of boring - endless reflection over the recent pain I've been feeling and healing.

Part of my issue is I feel like there is a vast chasm between those who vibe with my message and those who don't. A lot of my essay topics seem to be about convincing those who don't vibe why they should, but it feels futile to me.

Being home is definitely strange... it is so familiar. It is still home, for sure. I felt it tonight, sitting on the couch, eating dinner and watching a movie. Something about that... I remember when my grandfather, my jiddu. When my jiddu and teta would come, they tended to stay for months at a time. Teta upstairs in the office room adjacent to my bedroom, sharing my bathroom; I was always mystefied by the empty yogurt tub with the cloth. When they arrived from Syria, they brought with them a very particular smell. It was strong, cuminy, and it filled the room as they opened their luggage to reveal the baklawa.

My jiddu would stay downstairs, and on some nights we would eat dinner and watch a movie downstairs. It would be Charlie Chaplin's Modern Times, or The Great Dictator.

Tonight felt like that, with my dad bedridden after his hospital visit. There is a certain order and unspoken understanding that we have, the three of us. A system. The tightness of the immigrant only-child family system. It is strange to enter the system again... a role that is unchanged, even as I start to become their caretaker.

There is an intimacy and presence that I appreciate about them. Sometimes it can be overwhelming, or downright intrusive. Though, as an adult it largely feels more like I'm entering their reality than necessarily them entering mine. It's that little family universe thing.

To me family is something that each individual person has, potentially independently of the other people in their family: overlapping but not the same. Meia is in my family, I am in hers. My parents are in my family, and I am in theirs. I do wonder what happens at the end of the tunnel though... at least through my parent's eyes, it feels like a big point of family is to take care of you in old age. It's very simply the set of people you love, and that may not be the same as who those people love, and that is natural.

On ambition and imagination. Definitely working in corporate America is stifling my imagination. I feel... like my belief of the scope of what I'm capable of is narrowing. It's a battle to look beyond that. I do appreciate my dad's imagination and ambition. I'm grateful I have some of that, but also that it's more tempered and moderated.

My relationship with technology, with being an engineer, is kind of in a similar place as my relationship to reading, in a way. That feels... sad. I do feel excited when I start researching ways to carve my own path out. Software feels kind of constrained in that there's a limited imagination. I do like algorithms, and I'm more of a math person. And I liked computers growing up. So it all made sense... but I do have some regret not going further in the like research EE or other kind of deep science and engineering route. But really I think the thing to focus on is finding ways to feed my curiosity and desire to learn, so that can thrive.

The political and cultural aspects feel pretty fucked, at the same time. Is it really hope, or is it an even deeper cynicism, to say that the current system cannot be iterated upon? Well, I do think we need to work with what we have, but we need ambition. An ambitious wave of change would probably look like:

* Electoral reform on multiple levels
  * Ranked choice voting, no first-past-the-post
  * No campaign financing - public funds only.
  * Shorter elections
  * End Citizens United
* Massively slash defense budget, replacing with social programs
* Massive increase in wealth and business taxes on the higher end (tax large corporates more than small businesses)
* Housing, transportation, healthcare, education
* Stop oil subsidies, green the economy. Green New Deal.
* Judicial reform
* Immigration reform

That isn't even really that radical. That's basically just returning to new deal stuff. The issue is that it feels like it'll never happen: the planet will be destroyed (is being destroyed) before we even make the smallest adjustment in the course of this massive ship.

It's interesting that my dad seems to have so many ideas... but none of them seem to pan out. And yet he continues unfazed. I admire his willingness to stick to his guns, to be confident in his ideas and justify them. But... at the same time, adjusting and responding to feedback and adapting your idea in response to new information.

Anark's take on anarchoprimitavism... yeah. I hear what he is saying and think I'm largely in agreement. I think my interest in primativism is more about returning to fundamental technologies that we've abandoned for shinier things at great cost. It is definitely true that there is no going back at this point... I guess I needed someone other than Daniel to make that argument, though.

What a different environment Meia and I grew up in... this house is so very quiet. In comparison to the large community Meia was raised in, it couldn't be more different. I grew up in a terrarium, taken from my roots and placed in a new environment. There is a way in which that isn't true, because I grew up here, and a way in which that is true, because we did not have the community that is historically normal to have around you growing up.
